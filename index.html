01 — How you are marked

Four judges, equal votes.

Each criterion is 25%. Most students fail because they obsess over vocabulary and ignore Task Response and Cohesion — the two easiest to fix.

🎯

Task Response

Did you answer exactly what was asked with a clear position throughout?

  • Paraphrase + thesis in intro
  • Extend every idea, don't list
  • Clear opinion if asked
25% • TR
🔗

Coherence & Cohesion

Can a tired examiner follow you without re-reading?

  • One central idea per paragraph
  • Linkers + referencing (this, these)
  • Logical progression
25% • CC
📚

Lexical Resource

Precise, natural collocations — not rare words forced in.

  • Topic collocations
  • Less common phrases, used correctly
  • Correct spelling + word form
25% • LR
✍️

Grammar Range & Accuracy

Mix simple + complex sentences with few errors.

  • Complex: relative, conditional, passive
  • Punctuation matters
  • Errors must not block meaning
25% • GRA
02 — The five question types

Spot the type in 10 seconds.

Click each tab. Your structure changes slightly per type — get this wrong and Task Response caps at Band 5. Each card gives you: signal words, paragraph map, thesis templates, a model intro, and the trap to avoid.

1. Opinion — Agree / Disagree

★ Most common ~40%Easiest to master
“Some people think university education should be free for all students. To what extent do you agree or disagree?”
🔍 Signal words — spot it by:
To what extent do you agreeDo you agree or disagree?Do you think…?
✦ Model intro (41 words)
“In recent years, the cost of higher education has sparked heated debate. While some insist tuition should be entirely state-funded, I strongly disagree, as free degrees would strain public budgets and devalue qualifications.”
Sentence 1 background → Sentence 2 paraphrase → Sentence 3 opinion + 2 reasons preview.
⚠ Trap: sitting on the fence. “There are both pros and cons, it depends” with no verdict = TR Band 5. Pick one side. A qualified position (“I largely agree, although…”) is fine — but state it in the intro and never wobble.
📝 5-min plan example: Side = Disagree. Body 1: burden on taxpayers → Sweden/Norway cuts? Body 2 (stronger): devalues degrees → graduate oversupply, employers filter harder. Concession folded into Body 2, rebutted.
✕ Band 5 thesis
“This essay will talk about free university and give my opinion.” (vague, no position)
✓ Band 8 thesis
“I strongly disagree that… because… and…” (position + map)
📝 Sentence-by-sentence build — Opinion essay (15 sentences, 273 words)
  1. S1 — Background (general, no opinion)Set the scene in one neutral sentence.“In recent years, the rising cost of higher education has sparked heated debate.”Why: shows range with present perfect + collocation “sparked debate”. Never copy the question here.
  2. S2 — Paraphrase the questionRewrite the claim with synonyms.“While some insist that university tuition should be entirely state-funded…”“Some people think… should be free” → “some insist… entirely state-funded”. Proves paraphrase skill for TR/LR.
  3. S3 — Thesis: verdict + 2 reasons mapState your side + preview both body ideas.“I strongly disagree, as free degrees would cripple public budgets and devalue qualifications.”Examiner now knows your position AND the essay map (budgets = ¶2, devalue = ¶3). This one sentence locks TR Band 7+.
  4. S4 — Body 1 topic sentence (Reason 1, weaker)One clear idea only.“Firstly, fully subsidised tuition would place an unsustainable burden on taxpayers.”“Firstly” + “unsustainable burden” = CC topic signal + LR collocation. One idea per paragraph.
  5. S5 — Explain the mechanismAnswer “why is this true?”“Universities are enormously expensive to run, and the shortfall would have to come from general taxation.”Extends the idea instead of listing a new one — this extending is what separates Band 6 from 7.
  6. S6 — Deepen with consequencePush one step further: so what?“This would force cuts to healthcare and schools, or steep tax rises for ordinary workers.”Chain logic (free uni → tax rise → cuts) shows developed reasoning, not a list.
  7. S7 — Concrete exampleName a place, study, or number (plausible invention is allowed).“For instance, when Germany experimented with zero tuition, several states reintroduced fees after maintenance backlogs soared.”“For instance” + specific detail = TR extension. Keep it to 1–2 sentences.
  8. S8 — Link back to your positionClose the paragraph on-message.“Hence, universal free tuition is fiscally reckless rather than fair.”“Hence” + judgment echoing “disagree” keeps every paragraph tied to the thesis.
  9. S9 — Body 2 concession (one line, then pivot)Steel-man the other side briefly.“Admittedly, free access could widen participation among poor students.”One-line concession shows maturity (Band 8 TR). Keep it short — this paragraph is still YOUR side.
  10. S10 — Rebut + Reason 2 topic (strongest)Neutralise the concession, land your best punch.“However, this goal is better met by means-tested grants, because blanket subsidies mainly benefit the middle class; more importantly, free degrees erode the value of qualifications.”“However” + superlative “more importantly” signals your strongest card is coming.
  11. S11 — Explain why devaluation happens“When everyone holds a degree, employers can no longer distinguish ability, so they demand master's degrees for entry-level jobs.”Cause → effect chain. Present-simple + “so” clause = GRA range without risk.
  12. S12 — Example with result“South Korea, where over 70% of young adults graduate, now faces graduate unemployment above 10% despite near-universal degrees.”Country + statistic = credible support in one sentence. Don't over-explain data.
  13. S13 — Final link: tie both reasons to verdict“These credential inflation effects prove that ‘free for all’ helps few and harms many.”Echoes S3's “devalue qualifications” with new words — cohesion through paraphrase, not repetition.
  14. S14 — Conclusion: restate verdict + compress reasonsNo new ideas.“In conclusion, despite its egalitarian appeal, I remain convinced that blanket free tuition is unaffordable and counterproductive.”“Despite” concession + “remain convinced” restates S3 with fresh vocabulary.
  15. S15 — Close (recommendation, optional)One forward-looking line.“Targeted scholarships, not universal subsidies, are the fairer path.”Optional Band 8 touch — a recommendation, not a new argument.

¶1Paraphrase + thesis. End with your verdict + 2 reasons.45 w
¶2Reason 1 (weaker). Point → explain → example → link.95 w
¶3Reason 2 (strongest) + 1-line concession & rebuttal.95 w
¶4Restate verdict in new words. No new ideas.35 w

  • FULL AGREEMENTWhile some argue that X is harmful, I strongly agree that… because… and…
  • FULL DISAGREEMENTDespite claims that…, I firmly disagree, since… would… and…

2. Discussion — Both views + your opinion

★ Very common ~30%TR trap risk: high
“Some believe technology makes us more social, others think it isolates us. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.”
🔍 Signal words — spot it by:
Discuss both viewsDiscuss both sides…and give your opinion
✦ Model intro (44 words)
“Digital connectivity has divided opinion. Some claim social media deepens relationships, while others believe it erodes face-to-face contact. This essay will examine both perspectives before concluding that, on balance, the isolating effects outweigh the benefits.”
⚠ Trap: giving your opinion too early or only discussing one side. Body 1 must be fair to View A without “I think”. Save your verdict for Body 2's second half + conclusion. Skip your opinion entirely = TR capped at 6.
📝 5-min plan: Body 1 (View A — connection): video calls → migrants, grandparents; example: Zoom, migrant workers. Body 2 (View B + my stance): curated feeds replace real talk → loneliness stats; verdict: isolation stronger.
✕ Band 5
“I think phones are bad. Also some people like them.” (one-sided, opinion everywhere)
✓ Band 8
Body 1 purely View A, Body 2 View B + “In my view, the latter is more convincing because…”
📝 Sentence-by-sentence build — Discussion essay (15 sentences, 278 words)
  1. S1 — Background“Digital connectivity has transformed how people maintain relationships.”Neutral scene-setter. Present perfect shows GRA range immediately.
  2. S2 — Paraphrase View A“Some claim that social media and messaging apps deepen social bonds…”“Some believe technology makes us more social” → new words. Keep both views in one or two sentences.
  3. S3 — Paraphrase View B + outline (NO opinion yet)“…while others believe constant screen time erodes face-to-face contact. This essay will examine both perspectives before concluding that the isolating effects outweigh the benefits.”The golden discussion formula. Stating your verdict here is allowed (“before concluding that…”) — blurting “I think” in slang form is not. This phrasing keeps it academic.
  4. S4 — Body 1 topic: View A fairlyArgue the side you may disagree with, honestly.“On the one hand, supporters point out that technology bridges vast distances.”“On the one hand” + “supporters point out” = objective voice. No “I” in this paragraph.
  5. S5 — Explain how“Migrant workers can video-call their children nightly, and elderly grandparents can join family events virtually.”Two concrete images in one sentence — specific beats abstract (“people communicate more”).
  6. S6 — Deepen: why it matters“This continuity preserves emotional bonds that distance would otherwise sever.”Abstract payoff sentence (“preserves bonds”) lifts LR after concrete detail.
  7. S7 — Example“During the pandemic, for example, Zoom gatherings prevented complete social collapse for millions living alone.”Dated, verifiable example examiners recognise. One sentence is enough.
  8. S8 — Summarise View A objectively“Thus, advocates see digital tools as a lifeline rather than a threat.”“Thus, advocates see…” closes the view without endorsing it. Discipline = TR credit.
  9. S9 — Body 2 topic: View B + YOUR stance flag“On the other hand, critics argue that curated online interaction displaces genuine conversation, a concern I share.”“A concern I share” plants your verdict inside the topic sentence — exactly where examiners look.
  10. S10 — Explain the harm mechanism“Scrolling through highlight reels consumes hours that once went to neighbourhood talk, hobbies, and family meals.”Parallel list (“talk, hobbies, meals”) + contrast past vs present = strong CC + LR.
  11. S11 — Evidence / authority“A 2023 US Surgeon General advisory linked heavy social-media use among teens to doubled rates of reported loneliness.”Named source + number. Plausible invented stats are tolerated; absurd ones (“99.9% die”) are not.
  12. S12 — Why View B is stronger (evaluation)“In my view, this evidence is more compelling because virtual likes cannot replicate the empathy of physical presence.”“In my view… more compelling because…” = explicit evaluation. This sentence is the difference between TR 6 and 7.
  13. S13 — Concede View A's limit, reassert“While video calls help in emergencies, they are a thin substitute for daily community life.”“While…” concession + rebuttal in one sentence shows balance without fence-sitting.
  14. S14 — Conclusion: both views compressed + verdict“In conclusion, although technology connects the distant, I believe it distances the near, and the net effect is isolation.”Parallel antithesis (“connects the distant / distances the near”) — memorable, academic, and verdict-clear.
  15. S15 — Final implication (no new argument)“Schools and families should therefore protect screen-free time together.”Recommendation growing from your verdict, not a fresh idea.
PARAGRAPH MAP — 275 WORDS
¶1Paraphrase both views + outline. No opinion yet: This essay will discuss…45 w
¶2View A fairly (even if you disagree). 2-3 sentences + example.95 w
¶3View B + your stance. “In my view, this argument is more persuasive because…”100 w
¶4Conclusion with verdict. Summarise both, restate which wins.35 w

  • LEANING AWhile both arguments have merit, I believe the former view is more convincing because…
  • LEANING BAlthough X has some validity, I side with those who argue that…

3. Advantages / Disadvantages

Common ~15%Two sub-types — read carefully
“Working from home has become common. Do the advantages of this outweigh the disadvantages?”
🔍 Signal words — spot it by:
Do the advantages outweigh…?Discuss the advantages and disadvantagesIs this positive or negative?
⚡ Sub-type A (“outweigh / positive?”) = you must evaluate. Sub-type B (“discuss adv & disadv”) = balanced, no verdict needed.
✦ Model intro — outweigh type (43 words)
“Remote work has become the norm in many industries. While it offers flexibility, it also raises concerns about isolation. In my view, the benefits — higher productivity and inclusion — significantly outweigh the drawbacks.”
⚠ Trap: listing without weighing. “There are 3 advantages and 3 disadvantages” with no judgment = TR 6 max on outweigh questions. Use evaluation language: more significant, primary vs secondary, outweigh, while minor…
📝 5-min plan: Adv: no commute → productivity (Stanford 13%); inclusion → rural/disabled access. Disadv: isolation, blurred boundaries. Verdict: adv stronger → give Body on winning side more weight + sharper example.
✕ Band 5-6
“There are advantages and disadvantages. In conclusion both exist.” (no evaluation)
✓ Band 8
“Although isolation is real, it is secondary and solvable; the productivity and inclusion gains are structural.”
📝 Sentence-by-sentence build — Advantages outweigh essay (14 sentences, 271 words)
  1. S1 — Background“Remote work has shifted from emergency measure to permanent norm in many industries.”“From X to Y” structure + precise noun “norm” = instant LR credit.
  2. S2 — Paraphrase both sides“While it promises flexibility and lower costs, critics warn of loneliness and blurred boundaries.”One sentence, both sides, all paraphrased (“working from home” → “remote work”; “common” → “norm”).
  3. S3 — Verdict: which wins + why preview“In my view, the advantages — higher output and wider inclusion — significantly outweigh the drawbacks.”For “outweigh” questions the verdict MUST be in the intro. Dash-inserted preview maps ¶2/¶3. “Significantly outweigh” is the exact evaluation verb examiners scan for.
  4. S4 — Body 1 topic: weaker side (disadvantages) first“Admittedly, home-based work carries genuine drawbacks.”Putting the weaker side first lets you finish strong. “Admittedly” = academic concession marker.
  5. S5 — Disadvantage 1 + mechanism“Junior staff, in particular, lose the corridor conversations and mentoring that offices provide, which can stall their development.”Specific group (“junior staff”) + relative clause = developed, not listed.
  6. S6 — Disadvantage 2 + example“Moreover, when the kitchen table becomes the desk, many find it hard to switch off; a UK survey found 42% of remote workers report longer hours.”“Moreover” second point + semi-colon + data. Two disadvantages in one paragraph is fine — this is the weaker side, keep it tight.
  7. S7 — Concede-and-contain: why these are solvable“Yet these costs are manageable through hybrid schedules and right-to-disconnect policies.”“Yet… manageable” shrinks the losing side before the reader meets your winner. Key outweigh move.
  8. S8 — Body 2 topic: stronger side (advantages)“The benefits, by contrast, are structural and far-reaching.”“By contrast… structural” explicitly ranks this side above ¶2 — evaluation language, not listing.
  9. S9 — Advantage 1 + mechanism“First, eliminating commutes returns roughly an hour a day, allowing deeper focus and quieter, self-designed workspaces.”Quantified mechanism (“an hour a day”) + cause-effect. Spend more sentences here than on ¶2.
  10. S10 — Evidence for advantage 1“To illustrate, Stanford's trial of 16,000 call-centre staff recorded a 13% productivity rise among home workers.”Famous study examiners know — safe, credible, one sentence.
  11. S11 — Advantage 2: inclusion (stronger, moral weight)“Second, and more importantly, remote roles open employment to disabled professionals, carers, and rural workers previously shut out of city jobs.”“More importantly” + triple group list escalates significance — outweigh essays must argue importance, not count points.
  12. S12 — Why advantage 2 outweighs (explicit comparison)“Unlike occasional loneliness, which firms can mitigate, this widening of opportunity reshapes who can earn a living — a far weightier gain.”“Unlike X… this…” direct comparison sentence. Without it, TR stays at 6.
  13. S13 — Conclusion verdict restated“In conclusion, although isolation and overwork are real, I remain convinced the flexibility, productivity, and inclusion gains outweigh them.”“Although… I remain convinced” mirrors S3 with new adjectives. Both sides named, winner declared.
  14. S14 — Recommendation close“Employers should therefore formalise hybrid policies rather than force a full return.”Practical payoff flowing from the verdict — not a new argument.
PARAGRAPH MAP — OUTWEIGH TYPE
¶1Paraphrase + verdict. State which side wins immediately.45 w
¶2Weaker side (e.g. disadvantages). Acknowledge honestly.90 w
¶3Stronger side (longer, better example) + why it matters more.100 w
¶4Evaluation restated: “Therefore, … outweigh …”35 w
EVALUATION PHRASES — CLICK TO COPY
  • VERDICTIn my view, the advantages significantly outweigh the disadvantages because…
  • CONCEDE + OVERRULEWhile the concern about… is legitimate, it is secondary compared to…
  • BALANCED (sub-type B)This essay will outline the main benefits and drawbacks of…

4. Problem / Solution — Causes, Effects, Fixes

Less common ~10%Most predictable structure
“Traffic congestion is a growing problem in many cities. What are the causes, and what solutions can you suggest?”
🔍 Signal words — spot it by:
What are the causes / problems?What solutions / measures?What are the effects?
Variants: causes + effects, problems + solutions, causes + solutions. Count the question marks — answer all of them.
✦ Model intro (40 words)
“Gridlock now chokes most major cities. This is driven chiefly by car dependency and inadequate public transport. This essay will analyse these causes before proposing congestion pricing and transit investment as remedies.”
⚠ Trap: solutions that don't match your causes. If Cause = “too many cars”, Solution must cut car use — not “build more driving schools”. Examiners check the link. Mirror each cause with a fix.
📝 5-min plan: Causes: ① urban sprawl + cheap cars → car dependency ② underfunded buses → unreliable. Solutions: ① congestion charge (London −30%) ② dedicated bus lanes + subsidies. Each solution tags its cause.
✕ Band 5
Causes: pollution. Solutions: plant trees in schools. (mismatched, vague)
✓ Band 8
“Since the root cause is X, the most direct remedy is Y, as demonstrated by…”
📝 Sentence-by-sentence build — Causes & Solutions essay (14 sentences, 274 words)
  1. S1 — Background: scale of problem“Gridlock now chokes most major cities at rush hour.”Strong verb “chokes” + time marker “at rush hour” — vivid but academic. One punchy scene-setter.
  2. S2 — Paraphrase the task“This congestion stems from deep-rooted transport habits and planning failures.”“Traffic congestion… growing problem” → “congestion stems from…”. Shows you can nominalise (congest = noun).
  3. S3 — Outline both jobs“This essay will analyse two principal causes before proposing matching remedies.”“Will analyse… before proposing” promises Q1 then Q2 in order. “Matching” signals the cause↔solution link examiners reward.
  4. S4 — Body 1 topic: Cause 1“The primary driver is car dependency fuelled by sprawl and cheap credit.”Names the cause AND its roots in one sentence — depth from the start.
  5. S5 — Explain mechanism + effect“As suburbs spread, public transport becomes uneconomic, so families buy second cars, which flood arterial roads each morning.”“As… so… which…” chain: sprawl → no buses → more cars → jams. This causal chain IS the Band 7 skill.
  6. S6 — Cause 2 + effect“Compounding this, bus and metro networks in many cities are underfunded and unreliable, pushing even willing commuters back into cars.”“Compounding this” links cause 2 to cause 1 instead of listing. Present participle “pushing” adds GRA range.
  7. S7 — Evidence / illustration“In Jakarta, for instance, bus speeds average 12 km/h in peak hours, so ridership has fallen for five straight years.”City + number + trend. Concrete proof both causes are real.
  8. S8 — Body 2 topic: solution principle (mirror the causes)“Since both causes involve incentives to drive, the remedies must make driving costlier and transit better.”“Since…” explicitly ties solutions to YOUR stated causes — the matching examiners check for.
  9. S9 — Solution 1 fixes Cause 1 + proof“First, congestion pricing directly targets excess car use; London's charge cut central traffic by roughly 30% within three years.”Semi-colon joins remedy + proof. Named city + percentage = credible in 20 words.
  10. S10 — How to spend the revenue (feasibility)“Revenues should be ring-fenced for dedicated bus lanes and service frequency, addressing the reliability gap identified above.”“Ring-fenced” LR + “identified above” referencing back to ¶2 — textbook cohesion.
  11. S11 — Solution 2 fixes Cause 2 + guard against objection“Second, employers can stagger hours and expand remote days, flattening the peak; while this suits office staff best, even a 10% shift eases bottlenecks markedly.”“While…” concedes limits then quantifies impact anyway — mature, realistic proposals score higher than utopian ones.
  12. S12 — Link: why these work together“Together, the stick of pricing and the carrot of better options break the car-dependency cycle.”Idiom “stick and carrot” used correctly + “cycle” echoes S4's “dependency” — elegant closure of the body.
  13. S13 — Summary: compress causes + solutions“In summary, congestion is driven by sprawl-induced car reliance and weak transit, best answered by pricing plus transit investment and flexible hours.”One sentence, all four elements, no new ideas. Noun phrases compress 200 words into 25.
  14. S14 — Final note (no opinion needed)“Cities that pair charges with genuine alternatives will move fastest.”Forward-looking generalization — safe close for problem/solution essays where no opinion was asked.

¶1Paraphrase + outline: This essay will analyse… before proposing…45 w
¶2Causes / problems (2 causes, each + effect + mini-example).100 w
¶3Solutions matched 1-to-1 to causes + feasibility/example.100 w
¶4Summary of causes + solutions. No opinion needed.30 w

5. Two-part / Direct questions

Unpredictable ~10%Miss a part = TR 5
“Many young people leave rural areas for cities. Why is this happening? Is it a positive or negative trend?”
🔍 Signal words — spot it by:
Why…? Is it…?What…? How…?Two question marks
No standard instruction — the questions are the task. Common pairs: Why + positive/negative? What happened + what to do? Do you think + why?
✦ Model intro (42 words)
“Rural-to-urban migration among youth has accelerated worldwide. This is fuelled by better employment and education in cities. While this brings opportunities, I view the trend as largely negative due to rural decline and urban overcrowding.”
⚠ Trap: answering only one question, or answering in the wrong order. Body 1 = Question 1, Body 2 = Question 2. If Q2 asks “positive or negative?”, you must evaluate — don't just describe.
📝 5-min plan: Q1 (why): jobs pay more + universities concentrated in cities → example: Vietnam, 70% graduates stay in Hanoi/HCMC. Q2 (evaluation): negative — villages age, cities overcrowd → slums, pressure on housing.
✕ Band 5
Writes 250 words on “why” only, ignores “positive or negative?”.
✓ Band 8
“Turning to whether this is desirable, I would argue it is detrimental because…”
📝 Sentence-by-sentence build — Two-part essay (14 sentences, 272 words)
  1. S1 — Background: the phenomenon“Across Asia and Africa, villages are emptying as teenagers board buses for the capital.”Concrete image (“board buses”) beats abstract (“migration is increasing”). Hooks a tired examiner.
  2. S2 — Paraphrase Q1 (why)“This rural exodus is driven by the concentration of jobs and universities in a few megacities.”“Young people leave rural areas” → “rural exodus”. Answers Q1's core in noun form upfront.
  3. S3 — Preview both answers in order“While the pull of opportunity is understandable, I view the trend as largely negative for both villages and cities.”Q1 acknowledged (“understandable”) + Q2 verdict (“largely negative”) + scope (“both villages and cities” maps ¶3). Order preserved.
  4. S4 — Body 1 topic: answer Q1 directly“The foremost reason youngsters migrate is economic: cities simply pay more for the same effort.”“Foremost reason” signals Q1 has begun. Colon + blunt claim = clear, confident TR.
  5. S5 — Expand with contrast rural vs urban“A shop assistant in Hanoi can earn double her rural wage, alongside overtime and promotion prospects absent at home.”Specific job + city + “double” + triple list. Specificity is what makes examples believable.
  6. S6 — Second reason: education + social life“Added to this, top universities, cinemas, and hospitals cluster in capitals, so ambitious school-leavers feel staying means settling.”“Added to this” second reason without new paragraph — Q1 stays contained in ¶2. Alliteration “staying means settling” adds flair safely.
  7. S7 — Evidence“In Vietnam, for example, roughly 70% of provincial graduates remain in Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City after their degrees.”Country you know + plausible stat. Examiners accept invented-plausible data; they punish impossible data.
  8. S8 — Close Q1, bridge to Q2“These twin magnets of money and amenities explain the outflow; the question is whether it matters.”“Twin magnets” metaphor compresses ¶2; “the question is…” pivots to Q2 with perfect CC.
  9. S9 — Body 2 topic: answer Q2 (evaluation)“Turning to whether this is desirable, I believe the costs outweigh the gains.”Explicit bridge phrase + verdict. Examiner checking “did they answer Q2?” finds it in 12 words.
  10. S10 — Cost 1: villages hollowed out“Villages are left with the elderly and children, so farms go untended, festivals fade, and local schools close for lack of pupils.”Triple “so…” consequences in one sentence — developed effect, not a label.
  11. S11 — Cost 2: cities overcrowded + example“Meanwhile, arrivals crowd into slums and informal jobs; Manila's fringe settlements, housing over 600,000 newcomers, illustrate the strain on water and transport.”“Meanwhile” balances village vs city. Semi-colon + city example shows range.
  12. S12 — Concede the upside, contain it“Admittedly, some migrants remit money and gain degrees, but remittances rarely rebuild schools, and degrees seldom return.”“Admittedly… but…” + parallel “rarely/seldom” rebuttal. Concession handled in ONE sentence — Q2 stays negative as promised.
  13. S13 — Summary: one line per question“In summary, youth leave for better pay and prospects, yet this drains the countryside while overloading cities.”Q1 + Q2 each compressed to a clause, in original order. No new content.
  14. S14 — Recommendation (fits “what can be done” energy)“Governments should therefore seed rural colleges and broadband jobs to rebalance opportunity.”“Therefore” + concrete policy pair. Safe close even when Q2 didn't explicitly ask for solutions.

¶2Answer Q1 fully (reasons / what / why) + example.100 w
¶3Answer Q2 fully (evaluation / solution / opinion) + example.100 w
¶4Summarise both answers. One sentence per question.30 w

03 — The blueprint

Four paragraphs. Zero panic.

Examiners read hundreds of essays. Give them the shape they expect, then surprise them with ideas. Click each paragraph.

⏱ 5-min plan first: brainstorm 2 ideas per side, pick your 2 strongest, one example each (country, study, statistic — can be invented plausibly).
04 — Examiner's red pen

A Band 9 essay, dissected.

Toggle the highlight layers — this is literally what each band criterion rewards. Hover any highlight for why it scores.

In recent years, there has been growing debate about whether voluntary work should become a mandatory component of secondary education. While some argue that students are already overburdened, I strongly agree that unpaid community service should be required, as it fosters civic responsibility and improves employability.

Firstly, compulsory volunteering cultivates a sense of social responsibility that classrooms alone cannot instil. When teenagers assist at food banks or care homes, they are exposed to inequalities which are often invisible in their daily lives. This first-hand experience tends to nurture empathy and a sense of solidarity. For example, a 2022 study by the University of Melbourne found that students who completed 40 hours of service were 35% more likely to vote and volunteer as adults.

Admittedly, critics contend that such schemes place undue pressure on exam-year students. Although this concern is legitimate, it can be mitigated by scheduling service flexibly at weekends or during holidays. Moreover, community placements equip young people with practical competencies — teamwork, communication and time management — which employers increasingly value. These transferable skills often prove as decisive as academic grades in job interviews.

In conclusion, despite concerns about workload, I remain convinced that mandatory community service enriches both students and society. It bridges the gap between academic learning and civic life, producing more engaged and employable citizens.

06 — Don't bleed marks

The 6 Band-killers.

Each of these alone can cap you at Band 5–6. Check your last essay for them.

1
Memorised phrases

Examiners spot “This is a controversial hot topic in modern society” instantly. Paraphrase the actual question.

✕ In the contemporary era of globalisation…✓ In recent years, working from home has become widespread…
2
No clear position

Sitting on the fence in an opinion essay = TR 5. Pick a side in the intro and never wobble.

✕ There are pros and cons, it depends… (with no verdict)✓ While both have merit, I believe the advantages outweigh…
3
Listing without extending

Three half-ideas score less than two fully developed ones. Explain → example → result.

✕ Firstly pollution, secondly stress, thirdly cost…✓ One cause + why it happens + its effect + example.
4
Over-linking

Starting every sentence with Moreover / Furthermore looks mechanical. Use referencing too.

✕ Furthermore… Furthermore… Moreover…✓ This trend… These measures… Such policies…
5
Under length (under 250 words)

Automatic TR penalty — and you can't develop ideas in 180 words. Aim 260–290.

✕ 212 words = task not achieved✓ Count in practice. ~13-15 sentences ≈ 270 words.
6
Informal / emotional tone

No contractions, slang, or exclamation. Academic = calm and objective.

✕ Kids nowadays are lazy!!! Don't you agree?✓ Some argue that young people lack motivation…
